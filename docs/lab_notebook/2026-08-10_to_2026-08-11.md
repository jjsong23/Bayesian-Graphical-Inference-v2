## Objective

Over these two days, the Bayesian graphical-inference project was extended in
three related ways. First, node selection was formalized as an independent
binary present-versus-absent model with an objective prior probability of 0.5
for every candidate. Second, six collecting-duct abundance streams were added
for an Aqp2-focused analysis spanning CCD, OMCD, and IMCD. Third, the local GUI
and incremental edge cache were hardened so that the much larger
collecting-duct node universe could be submitted, monitored, cancelled, and
incrementally characterized without the browser losing the job or the Python
process materializing millions of pair objects in memory.

## 1. Independent binary Bayesian model for node selection

Node selection was revised to use the same two-hypothesis interpretation used
for edges. For candidate protein (i), the hypotheses are:

- (H_{1,i}): protein (i) is present in the signaling system; and
- (H_{0,i}): protein (i) is not present in the signaling system.

The objective default assigns equal prior probabilities:

$$
P(H_{1,i})=P(H_{0,i})=0.5.
$$

Each candidate is an independent Bernoulli hypothesis. Posterior probabilities
are therefore not normalized to sum to one across the 9,170 candidate
proteins. Evidence supporting one candidate does not reduce the posterior of
another candidate through competition for a fixed probability mass.

For evidence stream (s), a stored source likelihood was converted to a
neutral-relative Bayes factor. The source likelihoods for protein abundance
and principal-cell RNA range from 0.5 to 1, so their Bayes factors are:

$$
BF_{i,s}=L_{i,s}/0.5.
$$

Thus, neutral or missing evidence gives (BF=1), and increasingly strong
positive evidence approaches (BF=2). Other node tables that already stored
neutral-relative multipliers retained (BF=1) as their neutral value. For
stream weight (w_s), posterior odds are:

$$
O_i^{\mathrm{post}}
=O_i^{\mathrm{prior}}
\prod_s BF_{i,s}^{w_s},
\qquad
P_i^{\mathrm{post}}
=\frac{O_i^{\mathrm{post}}}{1+O_i^{\mathrm{post}}}.
$$

At the default prior of 0.5, prior odds equal one. A protein receiving only
neutral evidence remains exactly at posterior 0.5. The default selection rule
retains proteins whose posterior is strictly greater than 0.5. The existing
generic selection was preserved: 871 proteins plus 20 curated second
messengers, for 891 total nodes.

The HTML number controls for the node and edge priors were also changed to
accept arbitrary decimal steps. This removed the browser-side restriction that
previously accepted 0.500001 but rejected an exact value of 0.5.

## 2. Collecting-duct node evidence for the Aqp2 analysis

Six separately selectable node-evidence streams were added by crossing two
assays with three collecting-duct segments:

- rat proteome abundance for CCD, OMCD, and IMCD; and
- mouse renal-tubule RNA abundance for CCD, OMCD, and IMCD.

The three segments within an assay remain separately selectable, as requested,
but are tagged with a common dependence group because they originate from the
same source experiment. The six streams are disabled in the generic GUI
default so the validated 891-node analysis remains exactly reproducible.

### Rat-to-mouse harmonization

The rat proteome contained 7,429 unique rat proteins. Identifiers were mapped
to mouse genes with Ensembl BioMart release 116 orthology data. Rat UniProt
accessions were used first, with official rat gene symbols as a fallback.
High-confidence mouse orthologs were preferred when present. When no
high-confidence mapping was available, retained low-confidence mappings were
explicitly flagged. Valid one-to-many and many-to-many mappings were retained
rather than reduced to an invented single representative.

This procedure assigned at least one named mouse ortholog to 7,107 of 7,429 rat
proteins. Of these, 5,892 had a selected high-confidence ortholog and 2,678
mapped into the 9,170-protein signaling-candidate universe. The 322 unmapped
rat proteins were retained in an audit table. When multiple rat proteins or
ortholog records contributed to one mouse candidate, the maximum segment
abundance was used; abundances were not summed.

### Background distributions and scoring

For every assay/segment stream, zero was interpreted as nondetection rather
than evidence against node membership. Zero and missing measurements were
excluded from the empirical background and received neutral evidence
(BF=1). The threshold (T_{75}) was the 75th percentile of all strictly
positive finite source measurements in the relevant assay segment, calculated
before filtering to the signaling-candidate universe.

For a positive abundance (x):

$$
L(x)=\max\left(0.5,1-\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}
\left(\frac{x}{T_{75}}\right)^2\right]\right),
\qquad
BF(x)=\frac{L(x)}{0.5}.
$$

The resulting thresholds were:

| Evidence stream | Positive background values | Zero source rows | (T_{75}) | Observed candidates | Non-neutral candidates |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Rat proteome CCD | 6,550 | 879 | 66,565.7131 | 2,391 | 521 |
| Rat proteome OMCD | 4,395 | 3,034 | 70,816.4444 | 1,643 | 325 |
| Rat proteome IMCD | 5,621 | 1,808 | 65,193.9136 | 2,128 | 476 |
| Mouse RNA CCD | 25,161 | 29,371 | 22.9 | 5,449 | 1,933 |
| Mouse RNA OMCD | 38,933 | 15,599 | 9.2 | 6,428 | 3,005 |
| Mouse RNA IMCD | 31,140 | 23,392 | 11.7 | 5,774 | 2,351 |

The RNA source contained 100 duplicated nonempty gene symbols represented by
203 rows. Duplicate RNA rows were aggregated by the maximum abundance for each
symbol and segment.

## 3. GUI job control and cancellation

The original GUI had no way to stop a submitted analysis without terminating
the server process. A cooperative cancellation system was added across the web
client, HTTP server, workflow engine, and incremental edge cache.

The interface now displays a **Cancel analysis** button while a job is queued
or running. Cancellation is checked between scientific stages, while scanning
source tables, while scoring protein–kinase relationships, and between SQLite
write batches. A queued job can be cancelled before it acquires the analysis
lock. A running job transitions through `cancelling` to `cancelled`; completed
cache transactions are retained, while an unfinished transaction is not
reported as complete.

Only one analysis can be active at a time. A second browser tab is directed to
the existing job instead of silently creating another worker. The current
active job can also be recovered after a page refresh or a transient loss of
the original submission response. Job messages now distinguish queued,
running, cancelling, cancelled, failed, and complete states.

## 4. Diagnosis and correction of the expanded-workflow failure

Enabling all collecting-duct streams and scaffold-mediated closure initially
produced the generic GUI message **Configuration needs attention** with the
detail **Failed to fetch**. The configuration was scientifically valid;
scaffold closure was not the source of the failure.

Two implementation problems were identified:

1. The server started the heavy worker thread before it finished returning the
   job identifier to the browser. Under the expanded workload, the browser
   could lose the submission response even though the server continued the job
   in the background.
2. Incremental characterization constructed the complete set of approximately
   5.1 million new unordered pairs in Python memory and later constructed a
   second complete list of database rows. This caused an observed working-set
   increase to approximately 1.7 GB before integration.

The server now returns the accepted job record before starting the worker and
provides a lightweight active-job recovery endpoint. Incremental pairs are
generated as a stream and inserted with `INSERT OR IGNORE` in batches of 5,000.
Each batch is committed independently, so a cancelled long run can reuse every
completed batch. Previously cached pairs are detected from SQLite and a fully
cached request returns without rescanning evidence. Cached factor rows are also
read with a database cursor rather than loaded into one complete Python list.

## 5. Protein–kinase relationship bottleneck

The expanded run next appeared to pause at **Scoring new kinase–protein pairs**.
The cause was repeated file processing rather than a KinasePredictor model
failure. For each newly selected protein, `target_phosphosites()` reread and
reparsed the complete phosphosite audit table. The expanded configuration
therefore attempted roughly 2,400 redundant reads of the same file.

The phosphosite audit is now loaded once per incremental run and reused for all
targets. Motif scores are cached by residue and centralized 13-mer sequence, so
identical motifs are not rescored. The GUI reports progress every 25 targets as
**Scoring kinase–protein relationships (X of 3,297 proteins)**. The scientific
rule was unchanged: only observed or curated phosphosites are scored, and each
site retains the globally top ten mapped KinasePredictor models.

## 6. Diagnostic validation

The exact expanded GUI configuration was exercised after the changes. These
measurements are software diagnostics, not a completed biological analysis:

- the HTTP submission returned a job identifier in 0.01 seconds;
- the workflow progressed through 3,297 protein targets and entered pair
  caching normally;
- the process used approximately 220–317 MB during the observed kinase and
  early pair-caching stages, compared with approximately 1.7 GB before the
  streaming change;
- the cache reported progress at `20,000 of 5,103,091` incremental pairs; and
- the diagnostic run was then deliberately cancelled so the GUI would be free
  for an investigator-run analysis.

The first completed expanded analysis will still take appreciable time because
millions of unordered pairs must be characterized. This is expected work, not
a lock. The cache is persistent, and subsequent runs reuse all previously
completed pairs. The diagnostic batches were retained for this reason.

The full GUI regression suite contained 23 passing tests after these changes.
The tests cover default reproducibility, exact 0.5 priors, all six
collecting-duct streams, the 3,316-node selection, streamed unique-pair
generation, scaffold closure, queued and running cancellation, and cache reuse.
SQLite `PRAGMA quick_check` returned `ok` after cancellation and batch writes.
No browser-console errors were observed during the final expanded submit/cancel
test.
